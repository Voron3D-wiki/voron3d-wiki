Multi-Material Upgrade (MMU) systems allow 3D printers to print with multiple filaments in a single print. These systems enable multi-color, multi-material printing and provide flexibility for complex models. Below is a list of popular MMU systems with links, images, and brief descriptions.

- **Key Features**:
    - Z probing with standard voron tap

## Filament Feeders / filament Multiplexers

### [ERCF (Enraged Rabbit Carrot Feeder)](/MMUs/ERCF/)
- **Description**: A community-driven MMU system designed for high reliability and extensive customization.
- **Key Features**:
    - Supports up to 12 colors/materials
    - Highly customizable with modular components
    - Extensive community support and documentation
    - Advanced filament path control for reduced tangling

### [PICO-MMU](/MMUs/PICO-MMU/)
- **Description**: A very interesting project by [Ihndo](https://github.com/lhndo), the creator of the [LH-Stinger](https://github.com/lhndo/LH-Stinger).
- **Key Features**:
    - Compact design for all printers
    - Supports up to 4 colors/materials
    - Open-source and customizable
    - Lightweight and easy to assemble
    - no kits avaliable

### [BoxTurtle](/MMUs/BoxTurtle/)
- **Description**: An open-source multi-material system that supports 4 spools (unsure if they can be daisy-chained). Includes an auto-rewinding system, and a kit can be purchased through LDO Motors.
- **Key Features**:
    - Supports up to 4 colors/materials (per boxturtle)
    - Auto-rewinding system for unused filaments
    - Open-source with detailed documentation
    - Designed for compatibility with Klipper firmware
    - heated enclsure optional
    - [TriangelLabs kit](https://s.click.aliexpress.com/e/_c4SETbL9)
    - [Formbot kit](https://s.click.aliexpress.com/e/_c2vSlE0F)
    - [Seleadlab kit](https://s.click.aliexpress.com/e/_c311Qr7d)

### Happy Turtle Lettuce Feeder
- **Description**: "HTLF is a lane-based filament changer for Klipper designed by ArmoredTurtle. It uses a camshaft to engage the selected lane, effectively making the requested lane a direct drive extruder (no slip to contend with). The “stock build” is for a four color system, though CAD is provided should you choose to expand it further." From Robert_Klotz
- **Key Features**:
    - Supports up to 4 colors/materials (per boxturtle)
    - Open-source
    - Works with AFC klipper Add On
    - Lower cost than BoxTrutle
- [TriagnelLabs kit](https://s.click.aliexpress.com/e/_onfSHaw)
- [Seleadlab  kit](https://s.click.aliexpress.com/e/_oCy7tdC)

### [Low Rider V2](https://www.printables.com/model/1592898-low-rider-mmu-v20-for-any-klipper-printer)

## Selection Guide

### Factors to Consider
1. **Number of Materials**
      - 3-4 materials: Anycubic MMU, PICO-MMU, Box Turtle, BTT MMU
      - 5-8 materials: Prusa MMU
      - 9+ materials: ERCF, TradRack

2. **Installation Complexity**
      - Easy: Commercial solutions
      - Moderate: PICO-MMU, TradRack
      - Advanced: ERCF, Box Turtle

3. **Cost Considerations**
      - Budget: TradRack, Anycubic MMU
      - Mid-range: PICO-MMU, BTT MMU
      - High-end: ERCF, Box Turtle

4. **Waste and Change Speed**
      - Filament changers (ERCF, BoxTurtle, AMS-style) purge on every colour change, which
        adds time and plastic on prints with many changes
      - Toolchangers and hotend changers (StealthChanger, Tap Changer, IDEX, or commercial
        machines such as the Bambu H3C, Prusa INDX and Snapmaker U1) waste almost nothing,
        but cost more per colour
      - See [the approaches](#the-approaches) in the commercial section below

5. **Maintenance Requirements**
      - Low: Commercial solutions
      - Medium: PICO-MMU
      - High: ERCF, Box Turtle

## Commercial Multi-Material Options

Commercial printers and add-on filament changers now use every approach covered on this
page. Comparing them helps you decide what to build on a Voron, because each approach makes
the same trade-offs whether it is a kit or a closed machine. The table below compares the
approaches. It is followed by printers with multiple hotends or tools, and then by
commercial single-nozzle filament changers.

### The approaches

| Approach | How it works | Waste per change | Speed per change | Material mixing |
|----------|--------------|------------------|------------------|-----------------|
| **Single nozzle + filament changer** (AMS, CFS, ERCF, BoxTurtle) | One hotend; filament is cut or retracted and the next one loaded | High: the nozzle has to be purged | Slow: tens of seconds to minutes | Limited: materials share one nozzle and one temperature |
| **Dual nozzle** (Bambu H2D) | Two fixed nozzles on one toolhead, each of which can be fed by a filament changer | Low between the two nozzles, high within each one | Fast between nozzles | Good for two materials (e.g. PLA plus support) |
| **Hotend changer: IDEX** (independent dual extruders) | Two complete printheads on separate X carriages; the idle one parks at the side | Near zero between the two heads | Fast | Good for two materials. Many IDEX printers can also print two copies at once (duplicate/mirror modes) |
| **Hotend changer: hotend swapping** (Bambu H2C, Bambu H3C) | The printer swaps hotends from a rack or dock instead of whole toolheads | Very low | Fast | Good: each hotend keeps its own material and nozzle |
| **Full toolchanger** (Prusa XL, Core One INDX, Snapmaker U1, StealthChanger) | Each tool is a complete toolhead; the printer picks one up from a dock | Near zero | Fast | Best: each tool can run its own material and temperature |

:::note[Specs change quickly]
These are summaries of launch specs and early reviews. Sources sometimes disagree,
especially on build volume and tool counts. Check the manufacturer's current spec sheet
before you buy.
:::

### Printers with multiple hotends or tools

#### Bambu Lab H3C
- **Type**: Hotend changer (Bambu "SwapTek"), the successor to the H2C's Vortek system
- **Released**: November 3, 2026, from $2,499 (Combo)
- **Key Features**:
    - Nine swappable hotends, each with its own filament path and heater. Hotends preheat in
      the dock.
    - Bambu quotes about 3.4 s for a mechanical swap and about 6 s for a full material change
    - Hardened nozzles for abrasive filaments, 350 °C hotends, 65 °C actively heated chamber
    - About 330 × 320 × 325 mm build volume
    - Ships with a new nine-spool AMS unit with RFID, auto-loading and filament drying
- **Notes**: Early reviews say real prints run only slightly faster than on the H2C/H2D. The
  gains come from fast, low-waste hotend swaps, not from top speed.

#### Bambu Lab H2C
- **Type**: Hotend changer (Bambu "Vortek")
- **Key Features**:
    - One fixed nozzle, plus a rack of up to six Vortek hotends that swap in on the other
      side of the toolhead: up to seven nozzles in total
    - Expands to 24 filaments with AMS units
    - 350 °C nozzles, 120 °C bed, 65 °C actively heated chamber
- **Notes**: Out of the box, the hotend rack holds nozzles of mixed sizes, so it is not a
  seven-colour printer until you buy more hotends.

#### IDEX printers
- **Type**: Hotend changer (independent dual extruders)
- **Description**: Two complete printheads, each on its own X carriage. While one prints,
  the other parks at the side of the bed.
- **Key Features**:
    - Two materials with almost no purge, for example a model plus soluble support
    - Duplicate and mirror modes print two copies of a part at once
- **Notes**: Limited to two heads. The second carriage adds width and moving parts.
  Several commercial printers use IDEX, but few Voron mods do.

#### Bambu Lab H2D
- **Type**: Dual nozzle
- **Key Features**:
    - Two nozzles on one toolhead, each of which can be fed by an AMS
    - Good for printing two materials, such as a model plus soluble or breakaway support
- **Notes**: Colour changes within one nozzle still purge like any AMS printer.

#### Snapmaker U1
- **Type**: Full toolchanger
- **Key Features**:
    - Four independent toolheads
    - Snapmaker quotes about 5 s per tool change
    - 270 × 270 × 270 mm build volume
- **Notes**: One of the cheapest true toolchangers. It is smaller and louder than the Bambu
  H-series.

#### Prusa Core One INDX
- **Type**: Full toolchanger (Prusa with Bondtech's INDX passive-tool system)
- **Key Features**:
    - Up to eight tools on a Core One
    - Passive tools: the motor and extruder stay on the carriage, which keeps the tools
      simple and cheap
- **Notes**: The full eight-tool setup is one of the more expensive options.

#### Prusa XL
- **Type**: Full toolchanger
- **Key Features**:
    - Up to five independent toolheads
    - Large 360 × 360 mm bed
- **Notes**: The longest-running consumer toolchanger, with a mature PrusaSlicer workflow.

#### E3D ToolChanger
- **Type**: Full toolchanger (motion system and tools)
- **Notes**: One of the first hobby toolchangers. It made the dock-and-pick-up approach
  popular, and open-source projects such as Tap Changer and StealthChanger use the same
  approach.

### Filament changers

These are single-nozzle filament changers (the "AMS style"). Most are tied to their maker's
printers, but they show where open-source units like BoxTurtle are heading: sealed boxes,
filament drying, RFID spool detection and chaining several units together.

#### BTT ViViD
- **Description**: A BigTreeTech MMU system that offers integration with their control boards and firmware. At this time, there is not much known.
- **Key Features**: 
    - Seems like a AMS style system with space for 4 spoos

#### Anycubic MMU
- **Description**: Anycubic's solution for multi-material printing, originally released with the Kobra 3 3D printer. It will soon be supported by the upcoming Anycubic S1 as well.
- **Key Features**:
    - Supports up to 4 colors/materials

#### Prusa MMU
- **Description**: An innovative add-on for Prusa 3D printers, supporting up to five filaments for multi-color and multi-material prints.
- **Key Features**:
    - Automatic filament loading/unloading
    - Integrated with PrusaSlicer for seamless operation

#### Bambu Lab AMS family
- **AMS**: The original four-spool enclosed unit. Several can be chained.
- **AMS Lite**: An open, four-spool version for the A1 series
- **AMS 2 Pro**: A sealed four-spool unit that also dries filament (up to about 65 °C)
- **AMS HT**: A single-spool unit for high-temperature drying and engineering filaments.
  It can be mixed with AMS 2 Pro units, for up to 24 slots on the H2-series printers.
- **H3C AMS**: The H3C ships with a new nine-spool unit with RFID, auto-loading and drying

#### Creality CFS
- **Description**: Creality's filament system for the K-series printers
- **Key Features**:
    - Four spools per sealed, desiccant-equipped box. Stacks to 16 colours.
    - Reviews report reliability still improving through firmware updates. Check TPU support
      before you rely on it.

#### Elegoo CANVAS
- **Description**: Elegoo's low-cost multi-colour system
- **Key Features**:
    - Four colours, each driven by its own motor
    - Handles 95A TPU
    - No filament drying and no documented way to add more units

#### Anycubic ACE Pro / ACE 2 Pro
- **Description**: Anycubic's filament changer for the Kobra series. See also the
  [Anycubic MMU](/MMUs/Anycubic-MMU/) page.
- **Key Features**:
    - Four spools per unit, with drying while printing (ACE 2 Pro)
    - Reports differ on how many units can be chained. Check your printer model.
