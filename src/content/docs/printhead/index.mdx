## X Carriages

The X carriage is the part between the toolhead and the X-axis rail. It rides on the
rail's carriage block, holds the ends of the X/Y belts, and gives the toolhead (and often
the probe) something to bolt to. It's easy to overlook, but the carriage decides which
toolheads and probes you can run, and it affects print quality at speed.

:::note[Which printers this applies to]
This section is about CoreXY Vorons with a separate X carriage: the **V2.4**, **Trident**
and **Legacy** printers. The V0 and Switchwire use different motion systems, and their
toolheads mount differently.
:::

### Why the carriage matters

**Toolhead compatibility.** Toolheads attach through a mounting pattern. Most current
toolheads, including [Stealthburner](/printhead/stealthburner/),
[DownDraft](/printhead/downdraft/), XOL and A4T, use the **Clockwork2 mounting pattern**.
A carriage that keeps this pattern lets you swap toolheads later without rebuilding the X
axis. A carriage that is built into one toolhead (or uses its own pattern) ties you to that
toolhead.

**Probe options.** Where the probe mounts depends on the carriage. Eddy current probes
(Beacon, Cartographer, BTT Eddy) need to sit close to the nozzle at a fixed height. A
carriage with a native mount avoids printed adapters, which add flex and can shift the
probe offset. Nozzle probes such as Tap *replace* the carriage. See
[Bed Leveling](/bedleveling/) for choosing a probe.

**Rigidity.** The carriage is the link between the belts and the nozzle. If it flexes,
that shows up as ringing, softer corners and lower input-shaper frequencies. Stiff
mounting to the rail block and the belt clamps matters more than the material alone.

**Moving mass.** Everything on the X carriage is moved by the A/B motors on every move.
A lighter carriage allows higher acceleration for the same input-shaper result. This is
also why some speed builds pair a light carriage with a lighter X extrusion and an MGN9
rail.

**Belt clamping and tensioning.** The carriage takes the full belt tension. Clamps that
slip or are hard to reach make it hard to set and match belt tension, which affects
dimensional accuracy and skew.

**Rail size.** The carriage is made for a rail size and block type (usually MGN12H on the
V2.4 and Trident). If you change the rail, you have to change the carriage too.

### Types of X carriages

| Type | Examples | Toolhead fit | Probe | Trade-offs |
|------|----------|--------------|-------|------------|
| **Stock printed carriage** | The X carriage that ships with the Stealthburner/CW2 release | Clockwork2 pattern | Built for the stock inductive probe. Other probes need a mount | Well documented and easy to print, but an eddy probe needs an adapter |
| **Official modern carriage** | [Chariot](/printhead/chariot/) | Clockwork2 pattern (SB, DownDraft, XOL, A4T) | Native Beacon/Cartographer-size eddy mount, plus BTT Eddy, Klicky and PINDA | Newer, so less tested by the community than the stock carriage. Trident and V2 only |
| **Nozzle probe carriage** | [Voron Tap](https://github.com/VoronDesign/Voron-Tap) | Front-mounted extruders such as Clockwork2, LGX and Galileo | The nozzle *is* the probe (optical switch). No Z endstop needed | Built around an MGN12H rail. The toolhead moves on its own small rail, which adds parts and some mass |
| **Toolchanger shuttle** | [StealthChanger](https://github.com/DraftShift/StealthChanger) | Many toolheads through per-tool backplates (SB, Dragon Burner, XOL, A4T and others) | Usually nozzle probing (StealthChanger grew out of Tapchanger) | A different class of build: multiple tools, docks and more Klipper setup |
| **CNC metal carriage** | Aftermarket aluminium or ultra-light CNC carriages (see the [V2.4](/printers/2.4/) and [Trident](/printers/trident/) hardware lists) | Usually the Clockwork2 pattern, but check each one | Varies | Stiff and often light, but check the belt clamp design and probe mounting before you buy |

### How to choose

1. **Start from the toolhead.** If you want to keep the option of changing toolheads,
   choose a carriage that uses the Clockwork2 mounting pattern.
2. **Decide on the probe.** Eddy current probe: a carriage with a native mount, such as
   [Chariot](/printhead/chariot/). Nozzle probing: Tap. Multiple tools: StealthChanger.
3. **Match your rail.** Check the rail size and block type the carriage needs.
4. **Weigh mass against stiffness** only once the first three are settled. For most
   builds, a well-printed carriage in ABS or ASA is not the limiting factor.
